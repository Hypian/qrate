Documenting a programme in three steps

  1. Describe the activity, who took part and when.
  2. Let participants decide what they want to share and how they are identified.
  3. Give each figure a source. Distinguish the activity you recorded from an outcome you can support.

When to update supporters

  1. Confirm reporting dates with your funder. Write down the audience and who owns each update.
  2. Schedule a field update while people and material are available.
  3. Gather records, quotes and approved photographs.
  4. Leave time for Finance and programme staff to review the draft.

Five questions to ask a creative agency

  1. Who will produce the work locally?
  2. How will you handle consent?
  3. Can we see relevant samples?
  4. Which editable or raw files are included?
  5. Who approves what, and when?

Four sections for an impact report

  1. What changed? State the period and the source of each figure.
  2. What did the programme do? Describe the activities and who took part.
  3. How was funding used? Check financial figures with the person responsible.
  4. What comes next? Identify the next steps and what still needs to be learned.

Three prompts for a field interview

  1. What did you do?
  2. What changed for you, if anything?
  3. What would you want someone who wasn’t there to understand?

Photography and consent checklist

  1. Explain where the images may appear and ask whether the person wants to take part.
  2. Agree how someone can raise a concern or request withdrawal. Explain the limits once material is published.
  3. Keep the context. Don’t stage distress or edit an image in a way that changes its meaning.
